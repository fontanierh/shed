Image 1 is our real conversation screen: match its layout, header, composer, dark navy ground and typography. Image 2 is our exact dither texture up close and the single most important reference: square pixels on a strict grid with thin dark gaps between them, lit by a noisy ordered-dither threshold so the density of lit squares follows the brightness, coloured along our beam ramp (deep navy blue, electric blue, cyan, pale cream, warm amber). Every particle in the animation must be made of exactly these grid-aligned square dots: no round sparkles, no lens flares, no glows that aren't dither, no soft blobs, no glitch, nothing cartoonish or toy-like.

The motion, elegant and expensive, like a luxury title sequence: on the calm dark ground, a few slender streams of these square dither dots descend at different unhurried speeds, each stream a short gradient of dot density and colour, sparse dim navy dots at the tail building to dense pale-cream dots at the head with a fine amber edge, all snapping to the same pixel grid as image 2. When a stream reaches the height of a conversation line, it turns and flows sideways along that line, and its dots settle into a band of dither shaped like the line; that band then refines, dither dissolving into smooth, crisp white text from left to right, like a halftone print coming into focus. The blue user bubble fills in the same way from blue dither, then the body text lines one after another, then the "Worked for" card, its hairline border drawn by a single moving head of cream dots. Leftover dots dim out cell by cell. Lots of dark negative space, restrained palette, impeccable timing with easing. The header and composer stay perfectly still.
