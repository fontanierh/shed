UI motion reference, about 3 s plus a 1 s hold, looping, for a phone conversation screen loading. The reference image is our exact dither texture up close and the single most important reference: square pixels on a strict grid with thin dark gaps between them, lit by a noisy ordered-dither threshold so the density of lit squares follows the brightness, coloured along our beam ramp (deep navy blue, electric blue, cyan, pale cream, warm amber). Every particle in the animation must be made of exactly these grid-aligned square dots, at the same scale relative to the text as in a phone screen where each dot is about 2 points: no round sparkles, no lens flares, no soft blobs, no glitch, nothing cartoonish or toy-like.

The screen: a dark navy phone conversation UI, a slim header at the top (back chevron, a session title, a small menu) and a rounded glass composer at the bottom. At the start the space between them is EMPTY: calm dark navy with nothing in it.

The motion, elegant and expensive, like a luxury title sequence: a few slender streams of these square dither dots descend through the empty space at different unhurried speeds, each stream a short gradient of dot density and colour, sparse dim navy dots at the tail building to dense pale-cream dots at the head with a fine amber edge, all on the same pixel grid. When a stream reaches the height of a conversation line, it turns and flows sideways along that line, and its dots settle into a band of dither shaped like the line; that band then refines, dither dissolving into smooth, crisp white text from left to right, like a halftone print coming into focus. First a right-aligned blue user message bubble fills in from blue dither, then three lines of left-aligned reply text one after another, then a rounded "Worked for 12s" card whose hairline border is drawn by a single moving head of cream dots. Leftover dots dim out cell by cell. Lots of dark negative space, restrained palette, impeccable easing. The header and composer stay perfectly still throughout.
